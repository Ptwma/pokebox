# POKEBOX — Story & Progression Bible

> Status: v1 (authored by Claude, owner: Icon) · Tone: mystery / science · Language: English in-game
> Personal fan project. Pokémon names/creatures belong to Nintendo / Game Freak / The Pokémon Company; everything below the canon line (region, people, the Echo phenomenon, Glyph) is original.

## 1. The one-line pitch
Every time a Pokémon is sent through a storage PC, a faint copy of its data is left behind. For thirty years nobody asked where those copies go. In the Veyra archipelago, they have started to wake up — and they are printing themselves onto cards.

## 2. Canon foundations (why it is logical inside the Pokémon world)
The story only extends things the canon already establishes; it never contradicts them.

| Canon fact | How Pokebox uses it |
|---|---|
| Poké Balls convert a Pokémon into energy/data and back. | The conversion is lossy in one direction: a microscopic "residue" of the pattern stays in the relay hardware. We call it an **Echo**. |
| Bill's PC storage system transfers Pokémon as data between boxes and regions. | Relay stations route billions of transfers. Veyra hosts **Relay Node 7**, one of the oldest, built on a remote archipelago for cooling and isolation. |
| Porygon is a Pokémon made of data and lives in cyberspace; Rotom can inhabit electronics. | Proves data-life is possible. Echoes are *not* Pokémon — but under the right conditions they can hold a shape, like a Rotom holds a machine. |
| Unown are tied to strange dimensions and form written glyphs. | Echo interference patterns look like Unown script. The Veyra ruins predate the relay and carry the same script. |
| Holographic/battle-simulation tech exists (Battle Factory rentals, Battle Frontier, Pokémon holograms in the anime/Legends-era research). | Pokébox Labs found that Echoes bond to a crystalline card stock (**Lattice card**) and can be *projected* as a battle-ready hologram. That is why card battles in this game look and hit like real fights while no real Pokémon is hurt. |
| Terastal crystals and Galar particles show that regional energies can amplify Pokémon. | Veyra's energy is **Resonance**: when many Echoes of the same type gather, they strengthen — the in-world reason packs are themed and why some pulls are "hits". |

## 3. The region: Veyra
A ring of islands around a flooded caldera. Relay Node 7 sits on the central island; its cooling vents keep the waters warm and misty.

| Area | Biome / types | Mood | Pack tie-in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lumen Harbor** (hub) | port town, Pokébox Labs HQ, card shops | warm, busy | all standard sets; Sugar Rush festival stall |
| **Mistvale** | wetlands, reeds, waterwheels | quiet, foggy | Mistvale Collection (Water/Grass) |
| **Sandreach** | dune shelf, glass-sand from old vent heat | harsh, bright | Sandreach Collection (Fighting/Fire) |
| **Starfall Observatory** | cliff-top observatory tracking relay signal bursts | eerie, cold | Starfall Collection (Metal/Psychic) |
| **Voltspire** | wind-and-lightning plateau feeding the relay | loud, electric | Thunder Surge (Lightning) |
| **Frostline Peaks** | high glacier where the relay dumps heat into the ice | silent | Frost Holo (Water) |
| **The Obsidian Rift** | the caldera floor, directly under Relay Node 7 | wrong | Ascension → Obsidian (Dragon/Fire/Darkness) |
| (night event) | any area after dark, Echo activity doubles | uneasy | Moonlight (Darkness/Psychic) |

Rule of the world: **an area's Echoes match its environment** (water Echoes pool in wetlands, fire Echoes in the vent-heated desert). That is also the rule for packs: a themed pack is literally sealed from Echoes harvested in that area, so its contents are mostly those types.

## 4. People
- **You** — new "Lattice Ranger" hired by Pokébox Labs to collect and stabilise Echoes. Look fully customisable (Trainer tab).
- **Dr. Ione Vale** — head of Pokébox Labs. Invented Lattice cards. Brilliant, commercially minded, genuinely believes Echoes are harmless data.
- **Rho** — your rival-partner, a courier who runs relay maintenance. Blunt, funny, secretly frightened of the relay.
- **The Archivists** — not villains: a small order of researchers who study the Unown ruins and believe Echoes are the *memories* of Pokémon and should not be sold. Their leader is **Warden Sable** (the Elite you already fight on the ladder).
- **Circuit Wardens** — the eight battle-ladder trainers become the region's certified card-battle judges; each guards one area.
- **Glyph** — original entity (not a Pokémon species): a being made of millions of coalesced Echoes. Speaks in Unown-like patterns. Final chapter centrepiece.

## 5. Story — 7 chapters
Each chapter has objectives that pull from **every** system (packs, binder, market, battles, exploration), so the whole game is one progression.

1. **Static in the Harbor** — Arrive in Lumen Harbor, get your Lattice licence. Cards in the shop windows flicker by themselves. *Objectives:* open 3 packs, talk to Dr. Vale, beat Youngster Joey. *Unlock:* Mistvale, first outfit.
2. **What the Fog Keeps** — Mistvale's Water/Grass Echoes are appearing without anyone opening packs. Rho finds a waterwheel etched with glyphs. *Objectives:* collect 10 Water/Grass cards, explore the wetland shrine, beat Lass Mira. *Reveal:* Echoes gather where the relay's cooling water flows.
3. **Glass Under Sand** — Sandreach dunes are fused into glass in the shape of Pokémon silhouettes. *Objectives:* complete 25% of any binder set, win 2 battles with Fighting or Fire cards, find 3 glass silhouettes. *Reveal:* the silhouettes match *Pokémon transferred through Node 7 decades ago*.
4. **Signal at Starfall** — The observatory logs bursts that spell words in Unown. Warden Sable (Archivists) asks you to stop selling hits. *Choice:* help Sable read the glyphs or help Dr. Vale shield the relay (both paths continue; changes dialogue and a cosmetic).
5. **Voltspire Blackout** — The plateau's lightning feeds the relay; when it spikes, every Lightning Echo in Veyra wakes at once. *Objectives:* open a Thunder Surge pack, win 3 battles in a row, stabilise 3 pylons in the 3D world.
6. **Frostline** — Heat from the relay is carving caverns in the glacier; inside, Echoes are forming *combined* shapes. *Objectives:* reach trainer level 15, own 3 Ultra-Rare-or-better cards, beat Elite Orin.
7. **The Obsidian Rift** — Under Node 7 the Echoes have merged into Glyph. It does not attack; it copies. Final battle is against a team built from **your own most-used cards**. *Resolution:* Glyph is neither released nor deleted — it becomes the relay's guardian, and the Rift opens as the endgame area (Ascension/Obsidian packs, Champion Lyra rematches).
   *Epilogue:* Dr. Vale changes the Lattice process so a card can be "returned" (the market sell action is canonised as returning the Echo to the relay).

## 6. Progression economy (how everything feeds everything)
- **Trainer XP** (existing): packs, battles, set bonuses, daily, **+ story objectives, challenges, exploration finds**.
- **Journey** (new tab): current chapter, objectives with live progress, story scenes.
- **Challenges**: 3 daily + 3 weekly rotating goals (deterministic per day/week) + lifetime milestones.
- **Cosmetics** (Trainer tab): hair, hair colour, skin tone, eyes, hat, top, jacket colour, accessory, trainer-card frame, title. Unlock sources: level, chapters, challenges, battle ladder, binder completion, vault packs. Never purchasable with coins (so progress stays meaningful).
- **3D world**: areas unlock with chapters; hold NPCs, glyph finds (collectibles), Warden duels (card battles), and Echo encounters (a wild Echo = quick card battle; winning grants a themed card).

## 7. Consistency rules (do not break)
1. Real Pokémon are never harmed in card battles — only projections of Echoes.
2. Echo types follow the environment they gather in; themed packs follow the area they were harvested from.
3. No new Pokémon species. Glyph is an entity, not a species, and is never catchable.
4. Poké Ball / PC canon is extended, never contradicted.
5. Dr. Vale and the Archivists are both partly right; neither is "evil".
